# Rocket CFD showcase data

Source: owner-supplied SOLIDWORKS Flow Simulation tab-separated volume and surface exports, Biconic.txt, Hemisphere.txt, Blunt.txt. All cases are the 120 mph (2112 in/s) external-flow condition. This is post-processing of solved data, not a new CFD run.

Each JSON contains a 240 × 100 uniform sample grid, covering the same 12 in axial by 4.7 in transverse crop relative to the rocket tail and centerline. The screen's horizontal axis is negative source Y; its vertical axis is positive source Z. The center-plane X and transverse origin are estimated from cylindrical no-slip wall samples; axial registration uses the reported CAD lengths and foremost wall samples. The field is reconstructed with three-dimensional piecewise-linear interpolation of finite volume and wall samples near the plane. Missing points are not extrapolated. Quantization: nearest integer in/s, interleaved speed magnitude, Vy and Vz, signed little-endian int16, base64 encoded; 32767 indicates no support. The common 0–2300 in/s color scale covers the source maxima.

The original STEP-derived GLB silhouettes are used for the solid overlay and tracer exclusion. Tracers integrate the in-plane velocity components from the stationary field, with display time slowed uniformly; they do not represent a transient solution or complete three-dimensional trajectories. Approximate geometry registration, original mesh resolution, interpolation and projection limit near-wall accuracy. The source study used a level 3/7 global mesh without a mesh-convergence study. The animation is a qualitative visualization of the solved fields, not a new validation or an exact reproduction of a native SolidWorks cut plot.

Regenerate: python scripts/prepare-rocket-cfd.py /path/to/exports assets/cfd (requires pandas, numpy, scipy). The raw input files are not published in this repository.

## V7 pressure preview

`pressure-v7.json` contains the original solved `Pressure [lbf/in^2]` export column, expressed as Pa relative to 12.3 psi (the nominal far-field static pressure). Each case uses the same 240 × 100 center-plane coordinates, registration and physical crop as the velocity field. Pressure is reconstructed by inverse-distance-squared weighting of the 12 nearest finite samples in three dimensions, with 1 Pa quantization; this differs from the velocity grid's piecewise-linear interpolation. The renderer applies the velocity field support and CAD mask to the pressure contours. The common contour range is −700 to +1700 Pa. Velocity tracers retain the original solved Vy/Vz components and a common uniform slowdown. Local pressure peaks and near-wall features are limited by the source mesh and interpolation; this is a qualitative post-processing visualization, not a new CFD solve. The raw exports remain on the owner's Drive.

## V9 three-dimensional tracer preview

The `*-volume-v9.json` files contain the original three solved velocity components reconstructed on a common 112 × 48 × 13 grid covering the same axial and vertical crop and ±1.75 in in depth. Values are 8-nearest inverse-distance-squared interpolations, quantized to 1 in/s. No additional CFD solve was performed. The renderer integrates all three components and tests against a separately voxelized CAD volume rather than the projected fin silhouette. Opaque rocket pixels occlude projected trajectories, but do not cause deletion. Particles have no age limit; they are recycled only when leaving the visible axial/vertical domain. If a proposed step is unsupported, enters a solid, or crosses the depth limit, integration retains the last valid point rather than inventing motion. The pressure contour remains the V7 center-plane reconstruction, with common color limits equal to the raw samples' extrema in the 3D viewing crop (−2733 to +1646 Pa relative to 12.3 psi); nonlinear color stops emphasize small deviations around freestream without clipping those limits. These are qualitative post-processed trajectories, with source mesh and interpolation limitations.
