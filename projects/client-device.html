INDEPENDENT STUDY / FEB-MAY 2026

Confidential Client-Sponsored Mechanical Design

I was the primary mechanical CAD contributor on a four-person independent-study team developing a client-sponsored prototype. To respect the client, this case study discusses the enclosure design, hardware integration, tolerances, and team presentation without revealing the product concept or proprietary details.

01 / REQUIREMENTS

Design around components that already exist

The physical components and interfaces imposed packaging, attachment, clearance, and access requirements on the enclosure. I balanced those constraints against available space, realistic manufacturing tolerances, and assembly needs without disclosing the device's specific function.

02 / CAD AND MEASUREMENTS

Use the real parts instead of trusting nominal dimensions

I created most of the SolidWorks models for the four-part housing and checked the dimensions of eight physical components with calipers. Measurements informed fit adjustments and press-fit features so the CAD geometry better reflected the hardware that would be assembled.

03 / CROSS-DISCIPLINARY DESIGN

Make room for other engineers' requirements

I collaborated with teammates from electrical and computer engineering to translate component dimensions, mounting interfaces, and access needs into mechanical requirements. I revised the SolidWorks housing to accommodate those requirements while keeping the physical build practical.

04 / OUTCOME

Recognized as a team accomplishment

Our four-person team presented the project at a student showcase, where it received recognition among independent-study projects. I handled most of the mechanical CAD, but the overall result depended on contributions from the entire multidisciplinary team.

05 / SHARING AND LESSONS

Protect the client's work

I have not publicly posted the client's detailed CAD or technical drawings because permission to share them has not been confirmed. The transferable lessons are mechanical packaging, measurement, tolerance decisions, and communication across disciplines.

Confidentiality is part of engineering professionalism.

This page intentionally omits the project purpose, native CAD, and client-specific drawings. The design process and my engineering responsibilities can be communicated without exposing material belonging to the client.
