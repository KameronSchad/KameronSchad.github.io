INDEPENDENT STUDY / FEB-MAY 2026

Client-Sponsored Device Enclosure

A four-part mechanical enclosure for a multidisciplinary device containing circuit boards, sensors, wiring, and a battery. The mechanical design had to fit the real electronics and remain practical to assemble.

MY ROLEPrimary contributor to mechanical CADTEAMFour students from mechanical and electrical disciplinesTOOLSSolidWorks, calipers, fit and tolerance checks
PROJECT MEDIADetailed photographs are not published yet.
01 / REQUIREMENTS

Design around components that already exist

The electrical design imposed physical requirements on the enclosure: board footprints, connectors, sensor positions, cable routing, and battery space. The housing needed to package these parts in a compact assembly while remaining manufacturable and accessible.

02 / CAD AND MEASUREMENTS

Use the real parts instead of trusting nominal dimensions

I created most of the SolidWorks models for the four-part housing and checked the dimensions of eight physical components with calipers. Measurements informed fit adjustments and press-fit features so the CAD geometry better reflected the hardware that would be assembled.

03 / CROSS-DISCIPLINARY DESIGN

Make room for other engineers' requirements

I worked alongside electrical-engineering teammates to understand component placement, space for wiring, mounting needs, and how parts would be serviced. This required balancing the mechanical design with constraints established by the electrical system.

04 / OUTCOME

Recognized as a team accomplishment

Our four-person team presented the project at a student showcase, where it received recognition among independent-study projects. I handled most of the mechanical CAD, but the overall result depended on contributions from the entire multidisciplinary team.

05 / SHARING AND LESSONS

Protect the client's work

I have not publicly posted the client's detailed CAD or technical drawings because permission to share them has not been confirmed. The transferable lessons are mechanical packaging, measurement, tolerance decisions, and communication across disciplines.
