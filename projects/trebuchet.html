Early engineering design · Team project

February 2025Year 1

Timberline Trebuchet

I proposed the design approach for our five-person introductory engineering team, adapted an idea from a Tom Stanton video to our available materials, helped fabricate components, and recorded much of our test data. Our team finished first in the class tennis-ball target challenge.

MY CONTRIBUTIONConcept selection, practical adaptation, manufacturing assistance and data recording
CONSTRAINTRecycled materials; no CAD used
TEAM RESULTFirst place in class competition
On this pageOverviewMy contributionResultsReflection

01 / Design selection & fabrication

Choose a promising concept, then make it practical.

I identified a design in a Tom Stanton video ↗ and proposed it to the team. My contribution was selecting the concept and working out how to adapt it to the constraints of recycled materials and limited resources, not inventing the original mechanism.

We did not use CAD on this project. I helped manufacture components, including work on the release pin using a lathe. Roles were fluid, so I contributed where the team needed help rather than holding a formal title.

MY ROLE

Concept selection → hands-on fabrication → testing

Several hours per week outside class, working alongside a five-person team.

02 / Experimental work

Record results rather than rely on guesses.

I collected a substantial portion of the test measurements in a notebook and transferred them into the team’s digital records. Repeated testing helped the team evaluate consistency before the final competition.

We were among the few teams to hit the castle opening, and our team took first place overall. I was especially proud of the resourcefulness of the design and its low counterweight requirement relative to other teams, based on my recollection. This was not a controlled energy-efficiency comparison.

TEAM OUTCOME

First place.

Documented in the team's original project write-up. Individual credit is distinguished from the team result.

03 / Looking back

What I would change today.

I would conduct more research before choosing a design, especially around material selection and energy losses.

I would define a consistent experimental protocol and measure uncertainty before drawing quantitative comparisons.

This early project helped build the practical design and testing habits I applied to later engineering projects.
